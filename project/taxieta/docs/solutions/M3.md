**Bug found while solving (keep this lesson):** `astype("category")` per frame assigns
category codes based on the values *present in that frame*. Training, validation and a single
serving request get different codes for the same zone. LightGBM happens to remap pandas
categories at predict time, but many models and exporters (ONNX, XGBoost with codes, your own
embedding layer) don't. Fixed vocabularies make features identical everywhere: train/serve consistency again.

## Reference results — what to look for
- **Sampling:** stratified has the smallest borough distance and keeps rare strata; convenience
  (first 5 days) distorts the weekday/weekend mix and misses late-month effects; simple random is
  fine on average but noisy for Staten Island/EWR.
- **Label window:** with a 15-minute window roughly half the trips have no label yet, and the
  labeled ones are much shorter than average. That's the same underestimation as Twitter's delayed ad clicks.
- **Weak supervision:** `LFAnalysis` shows coverage, overlaps, conflicts and (with labels)
  empirical accuracy per LF. On the fixture, `lf_fare_per_mile_extreme` turns out to be *wrong*
  on the few rows it fires on. That's exactly why you need a small hand-labeled set. The LabelModel
  beats majority vote on recall because it learns which LFs to trust.
- **Imbalance:** accuracy is useless (zero rule ≈ 98–99%). Compare **PR-AUC** first. Undersampling
  inflates predicted probabilities (mean predicted prob ≫ base rate), so its 0.5 threshold looks
  high-recall/low-precision. Class weights and focal loss trade precision for recall. Two-phase
  keeps probabilities closer to calibrated. Pick the threshold by **cost on validation**, never at 0.5 by default.
- **Active learning:** uncertainty sampling typically reaches a given PR-AUC with fewer labels than
  random, though early rounds can be noisy.

## Learning-log answers
1. **Which nonprobability method did the convenience sample resemble?** Convenience sampling
   (“whatever loaded first”), with a flavor of quota sampling if you capped rows per file. Bias:
   calendar composition. The first days of a month have a different weekday mix and may include
   holidays (New Year). Late-month patterns (paydays, events) are absent. Any feature/label
   relation that varies over the month is misestimated.
2. **Weak supervision vs more hand labels:** better when experts can write crisp heuristics,
   data is large, privacy limits who can see rows, or definitions change often (re-run LFs instead of
   relabeling). Worse when the concept is subtle or perceptual (heuristics can't capture it), when
   LFs would be highly correlated/noisy, or when you need a gold-standard evaluation set. You
   always need *some* hand labels to measure LF accuracy.
3. **Resample after the split:** if you oversample before splitting, copies of the same minority
   example land in train *and* test (leakage → inflated scores). If you undersample the test set,
   your metrics describe an artificial distribution. Evaluation must reflect production's true class ratio.
4. **Undersampling and probabilities:** yes. Training on a 1:1 ratio shifts the model's prior,
   so predicted probabilities are inflated relative to the real ~1–2% base rate (see
   `mean_pred_prob`). Rankings (ROC/PR-AUC) can survive, but thresholds and any use of the raw
   probability (expected counts, cost decisions) break until you **recalibrate** (Platt/isotonic
   in M5) or correct the prior analytically.
